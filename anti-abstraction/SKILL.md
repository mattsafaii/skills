# Copywriting Guide: The Anti-Abstraction Method

## Core Principle

**Abstractions in copywriting are compensation for something you lack.**

Every time you use vague, aspirational, or generic language, you're covering for a deficiency in understanding, differentiation, specificity, confidence, or courage. Good copy is concrete, specific, and defensible.

---

## The Five Types of Abstraction (and What They Hide)

### 1. Lack of Understanding

**What it is:** You don't actually know what problem you're solving, so you use vague language that sounds meaningful but says nothing.

**Common abstractions:**

- "Strategic innovation"
- "Drive business value"
- "Optimize performance"
- "Enhance productivity"
- "Improve outcomes"
- "Accelerate growth"
- "Transform operations"

**Why you use them:** You haven't talked to enough customers to know what they're actually struggling with. You're guessing at pain points instead of documenting them.

**How to fix it:**

1. Talk to 10+ customers/prospects
2. Ask: "What problem were you trying to solve when you found us?"
3. Document their exact words
4. Use their language, not yours

**Example transformation:**

- ❌ Abstract: "Help you drive strategic innovation"
- ✅ Concrete: "Stop managing vendors so you can ship the cloud migration, open new locations, and deploy devices"

**The test:** Can you delete words without changing meaning? If yes, you have abstraction.

---

### 2. Lack of Differentiation

**What it is:** Your product isn't actually different from competitors, so you use generic aspirational language that could apply to anyone.

**Common abstractions:**

- "Best-in-class solution"
- "Industry-leading platform"
- "Cutting-edge technology"
- "World-class service"
- "Trusted by thousands"
- "Comprehensive solution"

**Why you use them:** You haven't identified what makes you actually different, or you're afraid your differentiation isn't compelling enough.

**How to fix it:**

1. List your top 3 competitors
2. Write their positioning statements
3. Write yours
4. If you can swap company names and the statements still work, you have no differentiation
5. Find the one thing you do that they don't (or won't)

**Example transformation:**

- ❌ Abstract: "World-class enterprise infrastructure solution"
- ✅ Concrete: "One platform covering five vendor contracts. One contract. One partner who knows your name."

**The test:** Could your competitor say the same thing? If yes, it's not differentiation.

---

### 3. Lack of Specificity

**What it is:** You're afraid to commit to a concrete, measurable claim because it's easier to be vague than to be accountable.

**Common abstractions:**

- "Improve efficiency"
- "Reduce costs"
- "Increase revenue"
- "Save time"
- "Boost productivity"
- "Streamline operations"

**Why you use them:** Specific claims are risky. "Reduce costs by 40%" can be challenged. "Reduce costs" can't. But the vague claim is also worthless.

**How to fix it:**

1. Ask: "By how much?"
2. Ask: "For whom specifically?"
3. Ask: "Doing what specifically?"
4. If you don't have the data, get the data
5. If you can't get the data, don't make the claim

**Example transformation:**

- ❌ Abstract: "Improve efficiency and reduce costs"
- ✅ Concrete: "Consolidate 5+ vendor contracts into one with predictable monthly pricing"

**The test:** Can you measure it? If no, it's too abstract.

---

### 4. Lack of Confidence

**What it is:** You hedge your claims because you're not sure they're defensible. You know what you should say, but you're scared to say it.

**Common abstractions:**

- "Help customers succeed"
- "Enable better outcomes"
- "Support your goals"
- "Facilitate growth"
- "Contribute to success"

**Why you use them:** You're afraid of being wrong. "Help customers succeed" is safer than "Reduce churn by 40%" because the first can't be disproven.

**How to fix it:**

1. Gather proof: testimonials, case studies, metrics
2. Document specific outcomes for specific customers
3. Only make claims you can defend with evidence
4. If you don't have evidence, don't make the claim

**Example transformation:**

- ❌ Abstract: "Help technical executives succeed"
- ✅ Concrete: "Inc 5000 #1,003 (2025)"

**The test:** Could someone challenge this claim and prove you wrong? If no, it's too vague.

---

### 5. Lack of Courage

**What it is:** You're afraid to exclude anyone, so you try to appeal to everyone with broad, generic language. You won't commit to a specific audience because you might miss opportunities.

**Common abstractions:**

- "For businesses of all sizes"
- "Across all industries"
- "Everyone who wants to [generic outcome]"
- "All your [generic category] needs"
- "Flexible solutions for any use case"

**Why you use them:** Picking a specific audience means saying no to others. That feels like leaving money on the table. But trying to serve everyone means serving no one effectively.

**How to fix it:**

1. Identify your ideal customer profile (ICP)
2. Be specific: company size, roles, industries, problems
3. Accept that you'll repel some people
4. Write ONLY for your ICP

**Example transformation:**

- ❌ Abstract: "For businesses of all sizes looking to improve connectivity"
- ✅ Concrete: "For CTOs, VPs of Technology, and IT Directors at mid-to-large enterprises"

**The test:** Does this exclude someone? If no, it's too broad.

## Practical Tests for Identifying Abstractions

### Test 1: The Deletion Test

Delete every adjective and adverb. Does the meaning change?

- If NO → those words were abstractions
- If YES → they were adding real meaning

Example:

- "We provide innovative, cutting-edge, transformative solutions" → "We provide solutions"
- Meaning unchanged = pure abstraction

### Test 2: The Competitor Swap Test

Replace your company name with a competitor's. Does it still make sense?

- If YES → you have no differentiation
- If NO → you have real positioning

Example:

- "Acme provides world-class enterprise solutions" → works for anyone
- "Acme consolidates 5+ vendor contracts into one" → specific to Acme

### Test 3: The Measurement Test

Can you measure the claim? Can you prove it true or false?

- If NO → it's too abstract
- If YES → it's specific enough

Example:

- "Improve your business" → unmeasurable
- "Consolidate 5+ contracts into 1" → measurable

### Test 4: The Audience Exclusion Test

Does your positioning exclude someone?

- If NO → you're trying to serve everyone
- If YES → you have a real target

Example:

- "For anyone who needs connectivity" → excludes no one
- "For CTOs at mid-to-large enterprises" → excludes small business, individual contributors

### Test 5: The "So What?" Test

Read your claim, then ask "So what?" If you can't answer with a concrete outcome, it's abstract.

Example:

- "We're innovative" → So what? → can't answer = abstract
- "We consolidate 5 contracts into 1" → So what? → "You stop managing vendors" = concrete

## Step-by-Step Process for Writing Concrete Copy

### Step 1: Identify the Real Problem (No Abstractions Allowed)

**Questions to ask:**

1. What are customers doing RIGHT NOW that they hate?
2. What specific tasks take up their time?
3. What keeps them up at night?
4. What are they complaining about to their peers?

**Document answers in their exact words.**

Example:

- ❌ Abstract problem: "Need better infrastructure management"
- ✅ Concrete problem: "I'm spending 15 hours a week reconciling wireless bills from 3 different carriers"

### Step 2: Define Your Audience Specifically

**Be brutally specific:**

- Job titles (not "decision makers")
- Company size (not "all sizes")
- Industries (if relevant)
- Current tools/vendors they're using
- Budget range

Example:

- ❌ Abstract: "Business leaders"
- ✅ Concrete: "CTOs, VPs of Technology, IT Directors at companies with 200+ employees"

### Step 3: State What You Do in Concrete Terms

**Format: [What] for [specific outcome] for [specific audience]**

Example:

- ❌ Abstract: "We provide connectivity solutions"
- ✅ Concrete: "We consolidate five vendor contracts into one platform under one contract for IT directors"

### Step 4: Differentiate with Specifics

**Not what you claim to be, but what you actually do differently:**

Example:

- ❌ Abstract: "We provide better service"
- ✅ Concrete: "When you call, you talk to someone who already knows your name and environment—not a support queue"

### Step 5: Provide Proof

**Use:**

- Numbers (revenue, growth, customers served)
- Customer quotes (exact words)
- Case studies (specific outcomes)
- Certifications (concrete credentials)

Example:

- ❌ Abstract: "Trusted by leading enterprises"
- ✅ Concrete: "Inc. 5000 #1,003 (2025) | SOC 2 Type II certified | Partner to three national carriers"

## Red Flag Words and Phrases

**If you see these in your copy, you probably have an abstraction:**

### Adjectives That Mean Nothing

- Innovative
- Cutting-edge
- Revolutionary
- Next-generation
- Best-in-class
- World-class
- Industry-leading
- Award-winning (without naming the award)
- Trusted
- Leading
- Premier

### Verbs That Are Too Vague

- Optimize
- Enhance
- Enable
- Facilitate
- Accelerate
- Transform
- Drive
- Empower
- Leverage
- Maximize

### Nouns That Hide Specifics

- Solutions
- Platform (without defining what it does)
- Ecosystem
- Framework
- Strategy
- Value
- Innovation
- Excellence
- Quality
- Performance

### Phrases That Say Nothing

- "Drive business value"
- "Strategic innovation"
- "Digital transformation"
- "Operational excellence"
- "Customer success"
- "Best practices"
- "Thought leadership"
- "Synergy"
- "Alignment"
- "Visibility into..."

**Exception:** Some of these words are fine if they're modified with specifics:

- "Platform that consolidates 5 services" ✅
- "Platform for your business" ❌

## Concrete Projects/Outcomes to Use

When you need to describe what customers want to do (instead of abstract phrases like "strategic work" or "what they were hired to do"), use these specific projects:

**Current list (expand as we learn more from customers):**

- Shipping cloud migrations
- Opening new locations (specify number if known: "opening 20 new locations this year")
- Deploying devices (specify quantity: "deploying 100 devices sitting in the warehouse")
- Passing compliance audits (specify type if relevant: HIPAA, PCI-DSS, SOC 2)
- Reducing CapEx costs

**How to use:**

- ❌ "Focus on what you were hired to do"
- ✅ "Ship the cloud migration, open new locations, deploy devices"

- ❌ "Get back to strategic work"
- ✅ "Get back to the cloud migration your CEO keeps asking about"

- ❌ "Do your actual job"
- ✅ "Deploy the 100 devices sitting in your warehouse"

**Note:** This list should grow as we talk to more customers. When a customer mentions a specific project they're trying to complete, add it here.

## Worked Example: Tightening a Positioning Statement

### Evolution of a Positioning Statement

**Version 1 (Abstract):**
"Acme provides strategic infrastructure solutions that drive innovation and optimize performance for modern enterprises."

**Problems:**

- "Strategic infrastructure solutions" = meaningless
- "Drive innovation" = unmeasurable
- "Optimize performance" = vague
- "Modern enterprises" = doesn't exclude anyone
- Could apply to any IT vendor

**Version 2 (Less Abstract):**
"Acme provides a modular connectivity platform that helps technical executives stop managing vendors and start doing their actual job."

**Better, but still has issues:**

- "Doing their actual job" = sounds specific but isn't (what IS their job?)
- "Connectivity platform" = undersells what the platform does

**Version 3 (Concrete):**
"Acme consolidates five vendor contracts into one platform under a single contract. We help CTOs, VPs of Technology, and IT Directors stop managing vendors and ship cloud migrations, open new locations, and deploy devices."

**Why this works:**

- Lists exactly what the platform includes
- Names specific roles
- "Stop managing vendors" = concrete activity
- "Ship cloud migrations, open new locations, deploy devices" = concrete outcomes (specific projects customers actually need to complete)

## Writing Exercises

### Exercise 1: Abstraction Audit

Take any piece of your copy and:

1. Highlight every adjective in yellow
2. Highlight every adverb in blue
3. Delete all highlighted words
4. Does the meaning change?
5. If no, rewrite without the abstractions

### Exercise 2: The Competitor Test

1. Take your homepage hero section
2. Replace your company name with your top competitor's name
3. Does it still make sense?
4. If yes, you need more specific differentiation

### Exercise 3: The Evidence Test

For every claim you make:

1. Write down what evidence you have to support it
2. If you don't have evidence, either get evidence or delete the claim
3. Rewrite claims to match the evidence you actually have

### Exercise 4: The Specificity Ladder

Take any abstract claim and make it more specific 5 times:

Example:

1. "We improve efficiency" (most abstract)
2. "We reduce time spent on vendor management"
3. "We consolidate vendor relationships"
4. "We consolidate 5+ vendor contracts into 1"
5. "We consolidate five vendor contracts into one platform under one contract" (most concrete)

Use level 4 or 5.

## Final Checklist

Before publishing any copy, run through this checklist:

- [ ] Have I named a specific audience (not "businesses" or "companies")?
- [ ] Have I described a concrete problem they're experiencing?
- [ ] Have I stated what we do in measurable terms?
- [ ] Can I prove every claim with evidence?
- [ ] Would my competitor be able to say the same thing?
- [ ] Does this exclude someone (good) or try to appeal to everyone (bad)?
- [ ] Have I removed all red flag words (innovative, cutting-edge, etc.)?
- [ ] Can I pass the deletion test (delete adjectives without changing meaning)?
- [ ] Would a 12-year-old understand what I'm saying?

If you answer "no" to any of these, you probably have abstractions.

---

## Remember

**Every abstraction is compensation for something you lack:**

1. **Understanding** → Talk to more customers
2. **Differentiation** → Find what makes you actually different
3. **Specificity** → Get the data to make concrete claims
4. **Confidence** → Gather proof to back up your claims
5. **Courage** → Accept that you can't serve everyone

**The goal isn't perfect copy. The goal is honest copy that says what you actually do for whom.**

Concrete copy builds trust. Abstract copy builds nothing.
