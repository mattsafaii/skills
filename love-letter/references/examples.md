# Real Love Letters in the Wild

Three documented love letters, each landing a different way. The point isn't the medium (website, video, email) — it's the pattern each one runs.

## 1. Francine Tamakloe → Spotify (public website, 2017)

Francine was an events and marketing coordinator; her dream job was Spotify. She built a love letter at **FrancineForSpotify.com**.

**What she did:**
- **Brand alignment + personality + humor** in the intro: "When I was 12, I wanted to be the Alicia Keys of rap music… I realized that being a trap pianist was not my calling."
- **Her style in their artists' words:** quotes from artists that describe her, each with an embedded Spotify song from that artist. This is the Secret Sixth run perfectly — their media, their typography, their gradients, woven into her story.
- **Work as transfer, not résumé:** four facets of her current job that map directly to the open role. Not a portfolio dump — a fit argument.
- **Clear intent + brand metaphor:** "I want to join the band." / "With my attention to detail and your solid bass line, we can make beautiful music (and outstanding business results) together."

**The transferable pattern:** the URL is named *for* the target. This is the "custom-made" test in one line — FrancineForSpotify.com could not be sent to any other company, and that's why it landed. If she'd blasted it to a hundred companies it would have failed.

**Result:** hired at Spotify; 5.5 years across two stints, starting as an associate, ending senior marketing manager for North America.

## 2. Brad Hussey → Dan Mall (public video, ~weeks before the talk)

Brad builds email systems for creators. Instead of a cold email, he made **"I funnel hacked Dan Mall (and how I'd 40% his revenue)"** — 23 minutes reverse-engineering Mall's setup from the outside: subscribed to the newsletter, cataloged every offer, mapped the audience segments, rebuilt the whole system the way he would if hired.

**What he did:**
- **Signal 3, run perfectly:** he built the map and the demo — the first 10% — and could not finish any of it without Mall's inside knowledge. Mall had to ask "how."
- **Public and brave:** tweeted "I rebuilt Dan Mall's funnel. He doesn't know it yet, but the internet will soon." Almost didn't post it. The fear was real — and it's the tax that keeps competitors from doing it.
- **Genuine, not manipulative:** it worked on someone who *teaches sales for a living* and spotted every technique. Mall: "I wasn't in the market for what Brad does… but watching his video, the feeling that kept surfacing was, 'I want that.'"

**The transferable pattern:** nobody docks you points for not knowing what you couldn't know; they credit you for the thinking. Being wrong about some guesses didn't cost him — the effort earned the slack.

**Result:** Mall became Brad's client; they finished a first project together.

## 3. The agency owner → a design leader (private email, from Mall's coaching)

An agency owner Mall coached sent a love letter to his dream client — a design leader at one of the biggest brands in the world.

**What he did:**
- **Homework signal:** named the conference talk that leader had recently given and quoted the exact line that stuck with him.
- **A vivid true memory:** told a story about the brand's products from his own life — and that experience became a campaign idea he pitched.
- **Silence, then follow-up:** no reply → followed up three days later → crickets → **five days later** the leader wrote back with *his own childhood memory*, and asked for patience while he shared the campaign concept with his entire creative team.

**The transferable pattern:** 
- Private works as well as public. Choose the medium by the target, not by what's flashiest.
- A stranger's email about a memorable moment connected to a memory and **conjured a project from scratch that neither of them were thinking about weeks earlier** — that's the ceiling of the method.
- Silence at day 3 is not a no. The follow-up is part of the move.

## The patterns, distilled

1. **The custom-made test decides everything.** If it could go to anyone, it goes to no one.
2. **One named person, one idea, their brand.** All three letters are this.
3. **Public or private — pick by fit.** Website (Francine), video (Brad), email (owner). The medium is the second decision, never the first.
4. **The 10% artifact is the load-bearing piece.** Francine's page, Brad's rebuilt funnel, the owner's campaign idea — each made the future visible.
5. **Genuine beats clever.** It works on people who know exactly what you're doing.
6. **Effort is the message; fear is the tax.** Brad nearly didn't post. The owners who ship anyway are the ones who win.
7. **Follow up, then wait.** ~3 days to nudge, ~5 more to hear. Day-8 replies happen.
