# Copy Scripts

Verbatim scripts and patterns from Mall's "The 23-Year Mistake," plus the skeleton they slot into. Swap in Safaii's facts; keep the shapes. Copy is Matt's — these are frames and examples, he writes every final word.

## The page skeleton (top to bottom)

```
[Draft the middle before the top — start with the homework section]
1. Intro            — "Dear [Name], [one strategic sentence of shared ground]"
2. Homework         — the opportunity: owned problem + stat in their units + missed market + FOMO
3. 10% to life      — the artifact (mockup / sizzle / sample page)
4. Been here before — 2–3 hand-picked Safaii proofs at matching scale
5. Bridge + close   — "I run Safaii Studio, [one line]." → opportunity summary → ask + next step + contact
```

## The two-sentence note that opened the door

The entire 23-year story collapses into a warm note to a person with a connection. This is the *sending* move — the page is the artifact, the note is the ask to look:

> "Congratulations! And honestly? I'm jealous. You have my dream job!"

Pattern: **warmth + one honest line of candor.** It did not pitch. Three months later she pitched him.

## Intro (Signal 1)

One strategic sentence, then move. Shared-ground patterns:
- Parallel background: "[Their detail] — mine's similar: [my parallel]."
- A specific compliment that shows you actually paid attention: name the talk/line/work.
- Not: "I've admired [Company] for years." That's a form letter.

## Homework summary (Signal 2)

The one-line distillation of the whole business case. Mall's:

> "Fix the foundation once, and every product built on top gets faster, cheaper, and more consistent. Forever."

Pattern: **the cost of not acting + the compounding payoff, in ~20 words.** Write this line last; everything above it earns it.

## The opportunity question

Mall's:

> "Can we build a next-gen design system for the NBA and go in the fastest growing market in sports?"

Pattern: **"Can we build [the thing] for [Company] and go in [the opportunity]?"** — one sentence that welds the deliverable to the market move.

## Bridge sentence (Signal 4)

The been-here-before credential, in one line:

> "I run SuperFriendly, a design collaborative that helps enterprises stand up design system practices."

Pattern: **"I run Safaii Studio, [what it is, in client words], that helps [client type] [outcome]."** Then let the proof points carry it.

## The close (Signal 5)

Exact two-question shape:

> "Wanna build this together?
> How's [Friday, August 7] for a chat? — I don't want to assume you're in; I want to give you room to say no, even though I'm hoping you say yes.
> [email] / [DM channel]"

Rules:
- Closed-ended ask first ("Wanna build this together?") — yes/no, room to decline.
- Next step second, **specific and ~7 days out**, dynamic so the letter never goes stale.
- Contact last, zero friction.

## The brand-metaphor line (Secret Sixth)

Francine's close:

> "With my attention to detail and your solid bass line, we can make beautiful music (and outstanding business results) together."

Pattern: **their brand vocabulary, welded to your craft, with the business payoff in parens.** Cornball is fine when it's true — it only works if it's the way you'd actually talk.

## Follow-up cadence

- Send.
- **~3 days** of silence → one short follow-up (a nudge, not a re-send of the whole letter).
- **~5+ more days** → the design-leader story: the reply came five days after the follow-up, ~8 days after the first send. Silence at day 3 is not a no. Don't burn the letter with anxiety; don't pester.
- The fear while waiting is **the tax on this method** — and the reason competitors won't do it. That fear is the moat.
