## Berkeley Mono (product copy)

Love letter to the golden era of computing. Wears a UNIX T-shirt. Aspires to be etched on control panels in black synthetic lacquer. Adrian Frutiger visits Bell Labs. Gene Kranz's command. Operates with calibrated precision and has a datasheet. Typeface for professionals. Coalesces 1970s machine-readable objectivity with humanist sans-serif qualities. Purpose is to make the user productive and get out of the way. It's boring. It's good. Functional first. Comforting yet stern. Old school, understated, unfashionable, potentially timeless.

Ligatures (BT-001) — writing code should be precise and unambiguous; ligatures that merge or replace glyphs violate that. Precision ligatures keep parts distinct (`==` keeps the gap; `->` is stem + arrowhead).

Houston Mono (BT-002) — if Berkeley Mono is Led Zeppelin, Houston Mono is David Bowie. Robotic exoskeleton over a humanist armature. Pull back from sci-fi chamfer cartoonishness. Stay rational and legible for code.
