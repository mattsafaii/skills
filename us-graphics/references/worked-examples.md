# Worked critiques

Two marks the office put on the record. Use as calibration, not as a template to copy.

## FAIL — Dell, 2016 circle wordmark

Verdict: fail. Time test and silhouette test. Contaminant: 2016 even-stroke thinning, plus a generic circular plate.

What it is: Siegel+Gale 1989 slanted-E wordmark, put in a circle in 2010 (Lippincott), then uniformly thinned in 2016 (Brand Union / Lippincott accounts differ) for the EMC merger. Same drawing. Lighter weights. Circle still a circle.

Silhouette: a circle. In a row of twenty tech marks it is a roundel with four letters inside. The tilted E is the only non-generic move, and thinning it reduced the one thing that read at distance.

SNR: blue carries some ID, but structure does not. One-color fax / embroidery / 16 px: you get a ring. The ring is doing the work. The ring is generic.

Domain: a PC/server company with nothing of the machine in the mark. No chassis, no pin, no grid, no "direct from manufacturer" residue. A circle meaning "global."

Construction: the 1989 E is a real idea (turn the world on its ear). The 2016 job was a global stroke-weight slider.

Name: DELL still reads. Dell Technologies lockup is a caption, not a system.

Time: this is what 2016 looked like. Even reduction of stroke-weight everywhere. Dated the year it shipped.

Keep: the 1989 slanted E. Kill the circle. Restore stroke contrast. Let the wordmark be the mark.

Neil, 2026-08-05: "Dell 2016 logo version is the most 2016 thing ever: Even reduction of stroke-weight everywhere."

## PASS — RAMTRON F-RAM (package stamp)

Verdict: pass. Domain, construction, name, silhouette. Sits in the Wyman–C&G band on the structured-trad side.

What it is: ferrolectric RAM maker, Colorado Springs. Mark as it appears on the die/package: isometric faceted diamond (crystal / cell) + custom RAMTRON wordmark + part number as the third line of the lockup. Name of the product family is F-RAM.

Silhouette: the diamond is not a circle, not a shield, not a rounded square. Black fill still reads as a crystal, not a blob.

SNR: survives laser-etch on black epoxy in a field of other chip markings. That is a high-noise environment. It stays itself.

Reduction: the diamond still reads small. The wordmark is a bit fine for 16 px as a standalone app icon — but this was never an app icon. It was a package mark. Correct scale for the object.

One-color: it is one color. It was born one color.

Domain: a memory crystal drawn as a crystal. Niche symbology. Only this industry would put that mark on that object.

Construction: few planes, isometric, stampable. No gradients required.

Name: RAMTRON (portmanteau that sounds like a part) + F-RAM (the thing) + FM18L08-70-SG (the actual device). Mark, wordmark, name. The datasheet is the brand book.

Time: unfashionable. Could have been 1989 or 2009. Still looks like a company that makes a component.

Keep all of it.

The later marketing rendering (wordmark only, "TCXO Processor Companion", F-RAM as a side label, stock sky split, no diamond) is the neutered version. Neil, same thread: "Unfortunately, all good logos get neutered by modernity."

Do not "refresh" this by thinning the strokes and dropping it in a circle.

## SCORECARD — live USGC identity (DX-202)

Three layers: stamp avatar (crop), source packing-label sheet, site wordmark with no lockup.

Verdict: salvageable as a system. Fail if the stamp is treated as the primary logomark.

Keep: concentric lozenge, stacked U.S. GRAPHICS COMPANY, USGC, Phoenix as a pin, one hard ink, house slogan.
Kill: scalloped postage as outline, map-as-mark, slop-crop as the only public face.
Next: C&G lockup = lozenge + USGC. Wyman lockup = label system. Put one of those on the masthead. Stamp stays a commemorative sheet.
